AST Talk Move Reference
Re-voicing: "So what I hear you saying is..."
Opening the floor: "Who can add to what [student] said?"
Pressing for mechanism: "Why do you think that happens?"
Wait time: [7+ seconds of silence — counts as a move!]
Harvesting disagreement: "I notice two different ideas here — both are interesting..."
Facilitator: Assign each room ONE question. They go deep on theirs for 7 minutes, then share back 2–3 key findings in 60 seconds each.
Room A
Teacher Moves
"What does the teacher do when a student says something unexpected or off-topic?"
Look for: Does the teacher redirect immediately? Pause? Build on it? What does that communicate to students?
Room B
Whose Words?
"How does the teacher use student language in building the checklist — whose words end up on the chart?"
Look for: Does the teacher paraphrase? Keep exact student wording? Who speaks most? Whose ideas get taken up?
Room C
Developmental
"What would this look like for K–2? What would you keep? What would you change?"
Consider: Max 4–5 items, visual supports, oral co-creation, drawing as evidence. Where does the complexity need to shift?
Room D
Justice Lens
"How is this checklist connected to justice? Whose knowledge counts as 'what we learned'?"
Consider: Whose ideas made the checklist? What languages are represented? What counts as scientific evidence here?
Justice-Centered Element
Room D's question is the most politically courageous — and the most important. The checklist is a public record of what counts as knowledge in this classroom. That is never neutral. Facilitator: name this explicitly when Room D shares back.
