The 5 Essential Points
After seeing the word cloud — compare what the class emphasized vs. what's in this framework. Click each point to read more.
1
It's real
Climate change is happening and the evidence is overwhelming — rising temperatures, sea level rise, shifting seasons, extreme weather events. This is not a debate in the scientific community.
2
It's happening now
This is not a future problem. It's occurring in students' lifetimes, in their communities — changing where it floods, where it's hot, what animals live nearby, what food grows locally.
3
Humans are causing it
Burning fossil fuels is the primary cause — and this has been accelerated by colonialism, industrial capitalism, and structures that allow fossil fuels to dominate economic and political systems.
⚠ This is the most politically courageous point to teach

Discussion: "What does it mean that climate change is 'maintained through white supremacy culture structures'? How do you teach that to 3rd graders?"
4
It's urgent
The window for meaningful action is closing. 84% of youth ages 16–25 are at least moderately worried about climate change (Hickman et al., 2021). Students are already feeling this urgency — we should not minimize it.
5
Solutions exist
This is the hope point — and it is discipline, not optimism. Mariame Kaba: "Hope is a discipline." Paulo Freire's "Esperançar." There are proven solutions at every scale. Student action can be real action.
