Block 2 · Activity 2.1

Word Bank vs. Must-Have Checklist

Model critique — what's the difference and why does it matter?

⏱ 10 minutes  ·  Model Critique + Chat
🔍 Compare
✍️ Respond
🗣 Discuss
⚠ Scaffold A — Word Bank
Students are asked to explain why Englewood is hotter than Lincoln Park. They receive this scaffold to help them write:
Word Bank
Albedo
Concrete
Heat
Temperature
Surface
Solar radiation
Urban
Vegetation
What this requires: Students select and insert vocabulary words. The scaffold provides the terms — students do not need to understand mechanisms to succeed.
✓ Scaffold B — Must-Have Checklist
Students co-create this checklist during class, recalling what they learned throughout the unit:
Our Must-Have Checklist — Urban Heat Island
I explain how dark surfaces absorb more solar energy than light or green surfaces
I use at least two pieces of data we collected in my explanation
I connect to what residents told us about nighttime heat
I explain WHY some neighborhoods have more concrete than others
I suggest what could change to make the heat more equal
What this requires: Students must recall, reason, and connect. Each item is a relational statement — a claim about mechanism or evidence, not a vocabulary word.
Key Insight from Kang, Thompson & Windschitl (2016)
Must-Have Checklists are co-created scaffolds that "prompt students to show more of what they know" and "provide multiple linguistic entry points" — including translanguaging, drawing, labeling, and gesturing. They support explanation without replacing it.

Developmental

Risk Check
"What's the risk of making the checklist too detailed? What happens to student agency if every item is prescribed?"
Justice-Centered Element
The Must-Have Checklist is explicitly framed by Kang et al. (2016) as a linguistic equity tool. It disrupts the assumption that only written academic English demonstrates scientific understanding. Items can be shown through drawing, gesture, multilingual writing, and oral explanation — broadening who gets to "show what they know."
