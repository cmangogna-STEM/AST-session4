The Teacher-Facilitator's ONE Rule
You cannot give the scientific answer.

Your job is to press stakeholders to explain their reasoning, connect ideas to evidence, and surface disagreements — without resolving them. If you feel the urge to tell them the answer, use a talk move instead.
Your Goal: Use at Least 3 of These
Facilitation Checklist
Asked at least 2 stakeholders to share their perspective
Re-voiced a student idea without correcting it
Pressed for "why" or "how" (not just "what")
Invited a quieter stakeholder into the conversation
Named a disagreement productively (not resolved it)
Press Without Telling — Your Talk Move Toolkit
Re-voice — repeat back with a slight reframe
"So what I hear [frog/developer/student] saying is... Is that right?"
Press for mechanism
"Why does that happen? What's the scientific process that causes that?"
Open the floor
"Does another stakeholder see it differently? What evidence do they have?"
Harvest disagreement
"I notice the tree and the developer have different ideas. Let's stay with that tension..."
Ask for evidence
"What data or observations support that? Where does your knowledge come from?"
Wait time (7+ seconds of silence)
[Just count silently. Don't fill the space. This IS a move.]
After Returning to Main Room
Teacher-Facilitator Reflects First
"Share one talk move you tried — and one moment where you wanted to just tell them the answer. What stopped you? What made it hard?"
Then the Class
"When did the science and the justice feel like they were in tension? What happened in that moment?"
Whole Class
"What would students need to know BEFORE this meeting? What would come AFTER it to deepen the science explanation?"
Justice Lens
"In your meeting, who had the most power? Was that the same as who had the most scientific knowledge? Why does that distinction matter?"
