Before You Watch: 5 Justice Dimensions to Listen For
Nature-Culture Relations — Students speak for the cliff, ocean, animals as legitimate voices
✓
Families & Communities as Belonging — Community members are legitimate scientific and civic voices
✓
Power, Historicity & Futures — Who has power to decide? What happened to this place before?
✓
Broadening Languages of Science — Drama, gesture, role play count as scientific reasoning
✓
Interdependence & Care — Students listen and build on each other's ideas across perspectives

Facilitator — Pause the video when a student speaks for a non-human stakeholder (the cliff, the ocean, an animal). Ask the class: "What is this student doing scientifically? What is she doing as a citizen?" Then resume.

Whole Class Discussion Prompts
Non-Human Voice
"A student speaks for the cliff itself. What does this do for the science learning? What does it do for the justice learning?"
Who's at the Table
"Who is at the table in this stakeholder meeting? Who is missing? If you ran this unit, how would you change the roster?"
2nd Grade Surprise
"This is 2nd grade. What surprised you about what these students were able to do? What did you assume about 7-year-olds that this video challenges?"
Justice Commitment
"How does this video show 'Fostering Interdependent Nature-Culture Relations'? Find a specific moment — not a general sense — of that commitment in action."
